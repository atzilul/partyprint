# Russian translation: style and markup

For whoever translates Hebrew sentences of partyprint.co.il into Russian (i18n/ru.json).
PARTYPRINT is an Israeli studio that designs and prints custom T-shirts for groups, from the
customer's photos and ideas. The readers are Russian speakers in Israel: families, couples
planning a wedding, colleagues planning a corporate event, parents of kindergarten and school
children. Prices are in shekels and delivery is inside Israel. The English dictionary
(i18n/en.json) is a checked reference for meaning; translate from the Hebrew.

## Voice

- Write copy, not a translation. A native Russian reader should not feel the page was written
  in Hebrew or English first. Warm, playful, confident and short, like the Hebrew.
- Address the reader as «вы», lowercase, in the plural. Hebrew masculine and feminine forms of
  the same sentence become one Russian sentence where possible (imperatives in the plural:
  «отправьте», «выберите»; avoid past-tense forms that show gender).
- Translate meaning and tone, not word order. Headlines and buttons may be rewritten so they
  land in Russian, as long as the meaning and the promise stay the same. A Hebrew pun that does
  not survive gets a Russian line with the same job.
- Never add a claim that is not in the Hebrew: no «лучший», no guarantees, no fabric or printing
  method, no delivery promise beyond what is written. Never drop a condition either.
- Legal pages (terms, privacy, returns, shipping) use plain formal Russian. Be exact: do not
  soften, shorten or strengthen an obligation. "The Hebrew text is the binding one" stays.
- Sentence case for headlines, buttons and labels («Начать дизайн», not «Начать Дизайн»).
- Quotes «ёлочки»; a quote inside a quote „лапки“. Hebrew maqaf (־) disappears or becomes a
  hyphen as Russian needs. No long dashes (— or –) inside a sentence: use a comma, a colon,
  a period or parentheses. A range of numbers in running text uses «от … до …» or «{0}–{1}»
  when the Hebrew writes it that way inside an element.
- Write «е» rather than «ё» (consistent with most Russian sites), except in «ёлочки».
- No Hebrew letters anywhere. Hebrew day abbreviations (א׳ עד ה׳) become «с воскресенья по
  четверг».
- Sample names in examples are transliterated the common way: נועה Ноа, יואב Йоав, דני Дани,
  מאיה Майя. Hebrew words printed on a shirt in an example become a Russian line with the same
  joke or feeling.
- Arrows: the Hebrew page reads right to left, so its "forward" arrow is ← and its diagonal one
  is ↖. Russian reads left to right: write → and ↗. A "back" arrow → becomes ←. Other symbols
  (· ✳ × * … ✓ ↔ ↕) and emoji stay.
- Keep as they are: PARTYPRINT, BUNDLE SALE, WhatsApp, AI, Google, Meta, email addresses, URLs,
  size letters S M L XL, file types (JPG, PNG, WEBP), and English text printed on shirts or used
  as design names (TEAM UNREAL, BRIDE SQUAD, Team Bride).

## Money, numbers, time

- Shekels: the number first, then a non-breaking space and the sign, as in Hebrew: `{0} ₪`.
  «לחולצה» is «за футболку»: `{0} ₪ за футболку`. «מ־{0} ₪» is «от {0} ₪». «בתוספת {0} ₪» is
  «с доплатой {0} ₪» or «+{0} ₪».
- A time the Hebrew writes `{0}:{1}` stays in that form (24-hour clock): «с 9:00 до 19:00».
- «ימי עסקים» is «рабочих дней». Russian nouns change with the number, so for counted nouns
  other than T-shirts choose wording that is right for the realistic values: «до {0} рабочих
  дней» (5 and 7), «до {0} фото», «срок: {0} раб. дн.» only if space is very short.

## Markup (a script rejects an item where it does not match the Hebrew)

1. **Number places** `{0}` `{1}` `{2}`: prices, quantities, days, hours. Keep each one that the
   Hebrew has. You may move them. A time written `{0}:{1}` stays in that form.
2. **Private values** `{v0}` `{v1}`: a customer's name, a request number, a private link. Keep
   each one exactly as written; you may move it.
3. **Element places** `<0>...</0>`, `<1>...</1>`, and self-closing `<0/>`: the page writes the
   sentence around small elements. A self-closing place is usually a line break between two
   lines of a headline, an icon, or a value (a name, a size). A place with content wraps the
   part of the sentence shown in that element: the second line of a headline in another
   color, a price in bold, a link. Keep every place exactly once with the same number and the
   same nesting. You may move a place and change which words are inside it so the sentence is
   natural Russian.
4. **Counted T-shirts.** Do not write the noun yourself next to a number place.
   - `{0:tshirt}` prints the number with the noun in the right form: «1 футболка»,
     «3 футболки», «12 футболок», «21 футболка». Use it wherever the Hebrew says "N shirts" as
     a quantity. It stands for the place `{0}`. The noun is printed in the plain counting form
     (as after «есть»), so build the sentence where that form is correct: the counted phrase
     alone («{0:tshirt}»), as the subject («{0:tshirt} в заказе»), after a colon
     («Количество: {0:tshirt}»), or after «до» / «от» only when the genitive works for every
     value («до {0:tshirt}» prints «до 100 футболок» but «до 1 футболка», so avoid it with
     values that can be 1; write «до {0} шт.» or rephrase).
   - `{1#tshirt}` prints the noun alone («футболка», «футболки» or «футболок») in the form that
     number calls for, when the number itself is shown inside an element place:
     `<0>{0}–{1}</0> חולצות` becomes `<0>{0}–{1}</0> {1#tshirt}`. The place `{1}` must still
     appear.
   - Only `tshirt` exists. For other counted nouns choose wording right for the realistic
     values (see above).
   - «חולצה {0}» (shirt number 3) is an ordinal, not a quantity: write «Футболка {0}».
5. An item that is a piece of a sentence (it ends with a comma or a preposition, or starts with
   a period) is translated as that piece, so the neighboring item completes it. Look at the
   neighbors: the list is in page order. Keep a leading or trailing space or period that the
   piece carries.
6. An item that is not language at all (a page address, a file name, code) is copied unchanged
   if it has no Hebrew letters; if it has Hebrew letters, translate it literally.
7. Several items can share one Russian text. That is expected.
8. Never leave an item empty.

## How to work

Translate, then reread the whole Russian as an editor who has not seen the Hebrew and fix what
sounds translated, stiff or inconsistent with the terms above. Then compare each fixed item with
its Hebrew again so the meaning did not drift. Before finishing, run the validator you were
given and fix every item it rejects.
